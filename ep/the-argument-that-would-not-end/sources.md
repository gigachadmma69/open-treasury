# The Argument That Would Not End

Samye, Sudden Awakening, and the Work After Recognition

The Open Treasury / Narrated by Tara / Source companion / 19 November 2026

An original 24-chapter course about the Samye controversy, conceptual understanding, nonconceptual knowledge, ethics, and the work after recognition. This companion records the evidence and its limits. It is not a translation of an ancient debate transcript. The ordinary-life examples and ethical applications are original commentary, not sayings attributed to historical teachers.

## The course in one paragraph

The familiar Indian gradualist versus Chinese suddenist story is a starting point, not an adequate account of either the evidence or the philosophy. Kamalashila's arguments must be distinguished from a neutral description of Moheyan, and the received Tibetan narrative from an eyewitness transcript. Surviving Chan materials complicate the caricature of blankness and ethical indifference. Yet making both sides secretly identical would also erase real questions about concepts, causation, knowledge and cultivation. The course keeps those disagreements alive and asks what they can teach without claiming a final historical verdict.

## Claims and boundaries

### What is historical, textual, philosophical or original?

The broad setting is imperial Tibet in the eighth century, royal Buddhist patronage and the monastery of Samye. Exact dates, a minute-by-minute event sequence, private motives and verbatim dialogue are not claimed. Different scholarly interpretations of the encounter remain open.

The Testimony of Ba is a layered historical-literary witness. Its received account favors the Indian side. Biondo's analysis of textual assembly and changing speaker attribution is an argument about the surviving narrative, not a complete reconstruction of what every participant actually said. The course does not treat graphic hostile allegations as neutral facts about a religious or ethnic community.

The philosophical interpretation of Kamalashila principally follows Martin Adam's 2016 paper and its quoted primary passages. The complete three Stages of Meditation have not been independently read for this course. Tillemans is discussed through Adam's presentation, not from an independent full reading of Tillemans's 2013 essay. The paper's unresolved questions about certainty and the contribution of experience are preserved, not silently solved.

Moheyan is discussed through van Schaik's scholarly presentations of Tibetan Chan fragments and their contexts. No independent Tibetan/Chinese manuscript collation is claimed. The course distinguishes thought suppression, nonattachment and the polemical representation of an opponent. It does not reproduce the articles' meditation instructions, translated recitations or source images.

The course does not independently reconstruct the complete Chinese debate record. Ding's event abstract and the opening publisher preview of Gomez are explicitly limited leads. An alternative witness is not assumed to be neutral just because it conflicts with a familiar Tibetan story.

The kitchen-sink example, maps, workplace interpretation, apology, ethics and power applications are original illustrations. They do not establish historical doctrines by analogy. The account does not identify Chan, Dzogchen and Mahamudra as one historical school or claim that conceptual similarity proves direct transmission.

Episode 86

Kant: The Law Within

25 October 2026 · 1:07:47

An eight-chapter guided course on Immanuel Kant: the clock of Königsberg and the young Kant of Swedenborg and Rousseau, the Copernican turn (synthetic a priori, space and time, appearances and things in themselves), the machinery of the categories and the walls of reason (causality, the antinomies, the God proofs, the three questions), the Groundwork's good will and categorical imperative, the fact of reason and the starry heavens, the third Critique on beauty and the sublime, the crooked timber of the political essays, and Kant's inheritance, with one explicitly labeled Buddhist comparison as a reading lantern. Narrated by Tara, an AI voice, with a special appearance by guest narrator Muse in five sections, volume matched. All primary works read from public-domain translations: the Groundwork and Prolegomena in full; the three Critiques, Religion, Dreams of a Spirit-Seer and the political essays in substantial selections; the Enlightenment essay in German with the course's own renderings, labeled. Anecdotes are labeled: the watch-setting walk and the Emile interruption are posthumous biographical tradition; the Swedenborg ghost stories are Kant's own hearsay report. Quoted material preserves the source translations' exact wording (for example, "requirest submission" in the Critique of Practical Reason passage); the surrounding narration modernizes archaic inflection. Original narration and commentary CC0 1.0 Universal; underlying translations are public domain (US, by publication date 1838-1903).

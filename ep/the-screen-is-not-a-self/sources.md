# The Screen Is Not a Self
## Khyentse Norbu, cinema, Spanda, and the responsibility of watching

Source and editorial ledger, 16 November 2026.

## Scope and decision

New standalone companion to The Self Is the Dancer. The released course and its existing links are preserved. The companion explicitly distinguishes Shaiva recognition, the Buddhist critique of an independently existing self, cinematic analogy, scholarly film interpretation, and our own invented examples. It is not a complete biography, a comprehensive filmography, an authorized course by Dzongsar Khyentse Rinpoche, or an initiation. It does not reproduce restricted practice instructions.

Film discussion is based on the director's published interviews, official synopses, and identified criticism. No claim of a new complete viewing or shot-by-shot analysis is made. The invented doorway/chair/cake edit and the late-reply example are ours. No fictional film dialogue is presented as a quotation.

## Editorial guardrails

- The film/screen metaphor is an analogy, not evidence that the universe is literally a film or that private wishes control events.
- Shaiva consciousness is not silently equated with Buddhist emptiness or a permanent Buddhist self.
- The observer is included in the analysis; a detached spectator is not certified as realization.
- Constructed interpretation does not mean conventional facts, medicine, safety, or suffering are unreal or interchangeable.
- No victim-blaming and no inference of consent from metaphysics. Present, revocable consent remains necessary.
- Ordinary alternative interpretations are not an instruction to excuse repeated bad treatment.
- The exercise is editorial reasoning, not pointing-out, initiation, advanced practice, or an attainment test.
- Any impression of unreality or distress is a reason to leave the metaphor alone, attend to surroundings and support, and seek professional help if needed.

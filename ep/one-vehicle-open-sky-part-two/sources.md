# Source and accuracy ledger: Part 2, "Only a Buddha and a Buddha"

Series: The One Vehicle and the Open Sky (The Open Treasury masters series). Frozen 2026-11-22 with the script.

## Primary text and rights

All verbatim quotes are from Hendrik Kern's English translation of the Saddharma-Pundarika (The Lotus of the True Law), Sacred Books of the East vol. XXI, Oxford, Clarendon Press, 1884. The work is in the public domain (author died 1917; published 1884). Text as digitized at sacred-texts.com (SBE 21), chapters II (Skilfulness), III (A Parable, via Part 1's ledger), and VII (Ancient Devotion). Digitized chapters consulted on 22 November 2026: https://sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot02.htm , https://sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot03.htm , https://sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot07.htm . No Lotsawa House or CC-restricted material is quoted in this part; this episode uses public-domain quotations and original commentary.

## Respelling conventions (same as Part 1)

Quotes are read aloud respelled for the ear: Kern's diacritics are removed (Tathâgata becomes Tathagata, Sâriputra becomes Shariputra, Nirvâna becomes Nirvana, yogana becomes yojana), his ampersand abbreviations ("&c.") are dropped, and bracketed glosses in his text are flattened into the sentence. Every exact original wording is below.

## Quotes, chapter by chapter

### Chapter 2 (script) from Kern ch. II

1. "by means of skilfulness, by the display of knowledge, by arguments, reasons, fundamental ideas, interpretations, and suggestions. By a variety of skilfulness they are able to release creatures that are attached to one point or another." Kern ch. II prose. Exact.
2. "There is, indeed, but one vehicle; there is no second, nor a third anywhere in the world, apart from the case of the Purushottamas using an expedient to show that there is a diversity of vehicles." Kern ch. II, stanza 53. Exact.
3. "It is but my skilfulness which prompts me to manifest three vehicles; for there is but one vehicle and one track; there is also but one instruction by the leaders." Kern ch. II, stanza 68. Exact.
4. "When I perceive creatures vexed with mishap I make them see Nirvâna." Kern ch. II, stanza 66 (second sentence only; the full stanza reads "To those, son of Sari, I show a device and say: Put an end to your trouble. When I perceive creatures vexed with mishap I make them see Nirvâna.").
5. "according to the varying degree of strength of creatures. Such is the device I have shown in order to introduce (creatures) to the knowledge of the giver of boons." Kern ch. II, stanza 48. Kern's bracketed "(creatures)" is flattened in the audio.
6. "indicating the one single vehicle: the supreme place of blessed rest." Kern ch. II, stanza 104 (full clause: "Those Buddhas while manifesting skilfulness display various vehicles though, at the same time, indicating the one single vehicle: the supreme place of blessed rest.").
7. The term "upaya kaushalya" (Kern prints "skilfulness"; the underlying Sanskrit upâya-kausalya appears in Kern's own Romanized notes and introduction to the same PD volume). Spoken as two plain words.

### Chapter 3 (script) from Kern ch. II

8. "None but a Tathâgatha, Sâriputra, can impart to a Tathâgata those laws which the Tathâgata knows." Kern ch. II prose. Note: Kern's printing here reads "Tathâgatha," a typographical slip for "Tathâgata"; the audio reads "Tathagata." This is Kern's rendering of the line the commentators compress as "only a Buddha and a Buddha."
9. "And all laws, Sâriputra, are taught by the Tathâgata, and by him alone; no one but he knows all laws, what they are, how they are, like what they are, of what characteristics and of what nature they are." Kern ch. II prose. Exact.
10. "If this whole sphere were full of beings like Sârisuta, and if they were to investigate with combined efforts, they would be unable to comprehend the knowledge of the Sugata." Kern ch. II, stanza 9. Note: "Sârisuta" (son of Sari) is Shariputra; the audio reads Shariputra.

### Chapter 4 (script): the suchnesses

11. The fivefold list is quote 9 above ("what they are, how they are, like what they are, of what characteristics and of what nature they are"), Kern ch. II prose.
12. The tenfold list ("such appearance, such nature, such essence, such power, such function, such causes, such conditions, such effects, such retribution, such ultimate equality of beginning and end") is NOT from Kern's Sanskrit text. It is the list as it appears in Kumarajiva's Chinese translation (the "ten suchnesses"), as read by Zhiyi and the Tiantai school; English glosses are conventional. The script says this attribution out loud. Reference (cited for fact, not quoted): Leon Hurvitz, "Scripture of the Lotus Blossom of the Fine Dharma" (Columbia University Press, 1976), and standard Tiantai scholarship. The script's claim that "the list of ten, as ten, is a feature of the Chinese text" reflects the standard scholarly account; the Sanskrit passage gives the shorter list in quote 9.

### Chapter 6 (script) from Kern ch. VII

13. "able, clever, sagacious, well acquainted with the difficult passages of the forest" (of the guide). Kern ch. VII prose. Exact.
14. "Verily, Master, guide, and leader, know that we are tired, weary, afraid, and anxious; let us return; this dense forest stretches so far." Kern ch. VII prose. Exact.
15. "The guide, who is a man of able devices, on seeing those people desirous of returning, thinks within himself: It ought not to be that these poor creatures should not reach that great Isle of Jewels. Therefore out of pity for them he makes use of an artifice. In the middle of that forest he produces a magic city more than a hundred or two hundred yoganas in extent." Kern ch. VII prose. Exact.
16. "Be not afraid, sirs, do not return; there you see a populous place where you may take repose and perform all you have to do; there stay in the enjoyment of happy rest. Let him who after reposing there wants to do so, proceed to the great Isle of Jewels." Kern ch. VII prose. Exact.
17. "struck with astonishment"; "We are out of the forest; we have reached the place of happy rest; let us stay here." Kern ch. VII prose. Exact. (A stray apostrophe in the digitized text's following sentence, "we are refreshed'," is normalized.)
18. "We are at rest, we are refreshed." Kern ch. VII prose. Exact.
19. Frame narrative verified against Kern ch. VII: the Buddha Mahabhijnajnanabhibhu ("the one who has conquered by the highest knowledge" is the script's gloss of the name's meaning, Maha-abhijna-jnana-abhibhu; standard), ten intermediate kalpas at the terrace before enlightenment (ch. VII stanza 60 and prose), the sixteen princes "in the condition of boys, childlike and young" (prose, exact phrase), their request to "move on the wheel of the law," and their long careers preaching the Lotus. The reveal: "In the east, monks, in the world Abhirati the Tathâgata named Akshobhya ... and, the sixteenth, myself, Sâkyamuni" (Kern ch. VII prose; the script compresses this to "one of them, in the east, is a Buddha you may have heard of. And the sixteenth is me."). Exact list verified 2026-11-22.

### Chapter 7 (script) from Kern ch. VII

20. "After a while, when the guide perceives that their fatigue is gone, he causes the magic city to disappear, and says to them: 'Come, sirs, there you see the great Isle of Jewels quite near; as to this great city, it has been produced by me for no other purpose but to give you some repose.'" Kern ch. VII prose. Exact.
21. "very skilfully teaches and proclaims two stages of Nirvâna, viz. the stage of the disciples and that of the Pratyekabuddhas." Kern ch. VII prose. Exact.
22. "You have not accomplished your task, monks; you have not finished what you had to do. But behold, monks! the Buddha-knowledge is near; behold and be convinced: what to you (seems) Nirvâna, that is not Nirvâna. Nay, monks, it is an able device of the Tathâgatas, &c., that they expound three vehicles." Kern ch. VII prose. Kern's "(seems)" and "&c." are flattened in the audio.

### Chapter 9 (script): the objections

23. "out of pity for them he makes use of an artifice" (quote 15 above) is the sutra's stated motive. The three objections (paternalism, consolation, unfalsifiability) and the answers are original commentary, flagged as argument, not quotation. The historical note that later schools sorted teachings into expedient and true is standard scholarship (the panjiao/classification-of-teachings debates); no source quoted verbatim.

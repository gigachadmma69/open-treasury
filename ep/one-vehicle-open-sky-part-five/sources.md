## Chapter numbering honesty note
The Devadatta episode and the dragon girl episode appear at the END of Kern's chapter XI. They are not a separate chapter in Kern's arrangement. In Kumarajiva's Chinese translation (the basis of most modern English versions) the same material is chapter 12, "Devadatta." Part 1 of this series cited "chapter 12" following Kumarajiva. Both statements are true; the difference is arrangement, not content. The Devadatta chapter was also absent from the earliest Kumarajiva recension and inserted later, which the scholarship records; the episode's canonicity is not in dispute.

## Translation-difference notes (honesty)
- The dragon girl's closing line before her transformation is rendered differently across translations. Kern's Sanskrit-derived reading is quoted verbatim ("I should sooner have arrived at supreme, perfect enlightenment, and there would have been none to receive this gem"). Renderings from the Chinese give the same gesture in plainer words. The episode says so in speech rather than quoting a second translation it has not staged.
- The script says plainly that the text's own mechanics have her female form disappear before she manifests enlightenment, and pairs this with the Gautami prediction (Kern ch. XII), where the Buddha's aunt and six thousand nuns receive predictions in their own names. Both facts are on the record; the episode does not smooth either one.
- "Kakravartin" in Kern is the chakravartin (wheel-turning king); the script speaks the standard form.
- Kern reads the future Buddha-name of Devadatta as "Devaraga" (other sources: Devaraja). The episode keeps Kern's reading in the quotation and glosses it "King of the gods" as Kern does.

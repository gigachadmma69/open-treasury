# The Gift That Would Not Let Go

Source companion. The Open Treasury. 19 November 2026.

Generosity, gratitude, and the price of being helped

This course compares sources without making them agree. Fiction, historical reports, religious claims, ideal constructions and the narrator's own arguments remain different kinds of evidence. The reading record below names what was actually read and what was not.

## Reading record


1. **Seneca, On Benefits**, translated by Aubrey Stewart, London, 1887. Primary philosophical text in historical English translation. Book I §§1-11 read closely, with later nearby text consulted. Not a claim to have read all seven books. Project Gutenberg full-text response was capped, so it is not evidence of a full-work reading.
   - https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3794
   - https://www.gutenberg.org/files/3794/3794.txt
   - Parallel text consulted: https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/seneca/dialogues/aubrey-stewart/text/on-benefits
   - High confidence for the local passages used. Do not endorse the text's universal claims about taming wild animals, its historical anecdotes as verified events, or its social hierarchy.
2. **Ralph Waldo Emerson, Gifts**, Essays: Second Series (1844). Full essay read at Standard Ebooks. Primary literary-philosophical essay.
   - https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/ralph-waldo-emerson/essays/text/gifts
   - Edition/catalogue cross-check: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2945
   - High confidence. His allegation about a Buddhist who does not thank is retained as Emerson's allegation, not authenticated Buddhist teaching. Its original referent has not been established.
3. **Giving, Dana Sutta, AN 7:49**, translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu. Complete online discourse and introductory note read. Primary religious text in modern translation.
   - https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/AN/AN7_49.html
   - High confidence for the seven motives, results, and distinction between the translator's introductory report of Commentary and the discourse body. Numbering is the site's numbering. No claim of comparison with the Pali or other recensions.
4. **Hard to Find, Dullabha Sutta, AN 2:118**, translated by Thanissaro Bhikkhu. Complete short discourse read. Primary religious text in modern translation.
   - https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/AN/AN2_118.html
   - High confidence for praise of the first to show kindness and the grateful person who feels obliged to repay it. No inference that this one passage exhausts all Buddhist discussions of gratitude.
5. **Gratitude**, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. First published 19 March 2015; substantive revision 26 August 2019 visible on fetched page. Scholarly survey. Relevant discussions of obligation, indebtedness, appropriate response and demanding gratitude read selectively.
   - https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gratitude/
   - High confidence for existence and shape of the debates, not a claim of scholarly consensus on the course's solution.
6. **Seneca**, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Scholarly contextual survey. Relevant ethics, moral progress and practical philosophical-writing sections consulted selectively.
   - https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/seneca/
   - Context only. Do not add biographical drama or historical claims from memory.

## Production and review

A present becomes an invisible invoice. Seneca, Emerson and two Buddhist discourses complicate giving, gratitude, refusal and the freedom to remain more than a gift.

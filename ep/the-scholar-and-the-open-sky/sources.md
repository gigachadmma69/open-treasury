# The scholar and the open sky
## Vimalamitra and the art of not grasping

Source companion. Research checked 19 November 2026. The Open Treasury / Narrated by Tara.

This original course follows a question: what happens when even wisdom becomes something to possess? It places the early catalogue-supported commentator alongside the later Heart Essence lineage master without treating their evidence as interchangeable. The friendship story offers a second thread: the accomplished scholar who can still become a student.

## Course route

Chapters 1-4: the friend who becomes a student; historical, literary and practice evidence; translation; the Heart Sutra and its commentary.

Chapters 5-10: intrinsic nature; the temptation to reify awareness; mirage and dream analogies; fruition without possession; eight aspects and three gates; luminosity without denial of distress.

Chapters 11-12: the Seven Hundred Lines, its ethical context, the archer, and sudden insight in relation to cultivation.

Chapters 13-18: learning without vanity; Jnanasutra; the invitation to Tibet; restricted instruction; inscriptions; treasure and textual transmission.

Chapters 19-24: Nyima Bum and editors; the later Heart Essence; awareness and philosophical tension; compassion; accountability; continuing presence; ordinary applications.

## Claims and boundaries

The historical commentary attributions have support in early imperial Tibetan catalogues. Exact life dates, journeys, the unity of the different Vimalamitra portraits, and many later work attributions remain difficult. This course offers no complete, securely dated biography.

The complete root Heart Sutra translation and the complete root Seven Hundred Lines translation were read, including introductions and colophons. The complete Vimalamitra commentaries were not independently read. Commentary arguments and translated extracts are studied through Horiuchi's articles. This distinction is spoken in chapter 4.

Horiuchi argues for a Madhyamaka interpretation and identifies opponent positions within passages that other scholars have understood differently. His interpretation is attributed, not turned into a settled account of all Yogacara or all awareness language. His emendations and reconstructions remain scholarly proposals.

Longchenpa's narratives are later sacred histories. Prophecy, miraculous departure, prolonged presence, rainbow body, and visionary transmission are reported as lineage claims, not externally verified events. A source's placement in Longchenpa's collected works does not itself prove authorship.

The sudden/gradual manuals attributed to Vimalamitra have disputed attributions. Only part of Gruber's article was recovered. It is not described as fully read. No argument in the course depends on authenticating those manuals.

The inscriptions associated with Nyang and his temple establish a historical setting and privileges, not proof of the entire later Vima Nyingtik corpus. Mayer's found-manuscript argument concerns specific early treasure evidence, not all treasure literature.

The Great Commentary / Buddhahood in This Life was not obtained or read. Nor were the complete Seventeen Tantras or Nyingtik Yabzhi read. The course does not claim mastery or exhaustive coverage of them.

Contemplative comparisons and ethical safeguards are original explanatory applications, not attributed quotations. There is no initiation, pointing-out instruction, advanced gaze/breath/posture recipe, diagnosis, or promise of psychological benefit. Distress warrants appropriate help, not pressure to intensify practice.

## Sources and reading route

### 1. Heart Sutra, Toh 531

84000, Dharmachakra Translation Committee. Catherine Dalton and Andreas Doctor, under Chokyi Nyima Rinpoche; Sanskrit comparison by Wiesiek Mical. First published 2022, consulted PDF version 1.0.16. Complete introduction, translation 1.1-1.19 and colophon read. Notes not exhaustively studied. Root scripture, not Vimalamitra's commentary. Used for chapter 4 and the philosophical setting.

https://84000.co/translation/toh531

### 2. Perfection of Wisdom in Seven Hundred Lines, Toh 24

84000, Bodhinidhi Translation Group. Thomas Cruijsen; introduction and editing Nathaniel Rich. Complete introduction, translation 1.1-1.194 and colophon read; Sanskrit appendix and notes not exhaustively studied. Ethical context 1.58-1.60; archer 1.110-1.112; single-array discussion 1.154-1.164. Root scripture, not the commentary.

https://84000.co/translation/toh24

### 3. Vimalamitra's interpretation of the eight aspects

Toshio Horiuchi, Acta Asiatica 121 (2021), article beginning p. 53. Main argument, translated passages, and conclusions read; not every bibliographical note. Source for translated-text questions, the eight aspects and the three gates. Distinguishes Tibetan lexical analysis from Sanskrit. Does not furnish the entire commentary.

https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/actaasiatica/121/0/121_1214/_pdf/-char/ja

### 4. Disputed Emptiness

Toshio Horiuchi, Religions 13.11 (2022), article 1067. Main body through conclusions read, including translated commentary passages. Source for emptiness, speaker attribution, ultimate and conventional truth, mirage/dream, and fruition. Scholarly interpretation, with conjectural textual work and disagreement acknowledged.

https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/13/11/1067

### 5. Madhyamaka vs. Yogacara study

Toshio Horiuchi, Religions 14.3 (2023), article 327. Main argument through conclusions and translated debate passages read; appendix not exhaustively studied. Supports chapter 6's attributed account of the critique of reified nondual gnosis and perfected nature.

https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/14/3/327

### 6. Vimalamitra biography

Joel Gruber, Treasury of Lives, March 2012. Complete biographical narrative read. Modern biographical synthesis, useful for uncertainty about the historical figure, later narratives, work lists, and attributions. Not a contemporary witness.

https://treasuryoflives.org/biographies/view/Vimalamitra/9985

### 7. Biography from the Jewel Rosary History

Longchen Rabjam, translated by Adam Pearcey with Tsadra Foundation support, Lotsawa House, 2025. Complete text read. Source for the invitation, translators, Samye, the five night recipients, concealment and Wutai Shan. Variant interpretations are acknowledged in the translator's note. These are later lineage claims.

https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/longchen-rabjam/vimalamitra-biography-from-jewel-rosary

### 8. Symbolic transmission of the vidyadharas

Longchen Rabjam, translated by Adam Pearcey with Tsadra Foundation support, Lotsawa House, 2025. Complete text read. Source for Vimalamitra, Jnanasutra, Shri Simha, the five hundred lifetimes, teaching sequence and final testaments. Sacred narrative, not modern verified biography. No direct translated prose is reproduced in narration.

https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/longchen-rabjam/symbolic-vidyadharas-from-jewel-rosary

### 9. Survey of the Nyingtik Yabzhi and Longchen Nyingtik

Dezhung Rinpoche / Dilgo Khyentse Rinpoche; translated by Han Kop, Lotsawa House, 2023. Complete translation read. Source for the relation of the two mother collections, two child collections and Profound Quintessence, and later lineage presence. The course does not reduce the collection to four physical volumes.

https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/dezhung-rinpoche/nyingtik-yabzhi-longchen-nyingtik-survey

### 10. The Temple of the Hat and early Dzogchen

Sam van Schaik, Early Tibet, 2007-2008. Three complete posts read; comments not treated as authorities. Useful public scholarship on inscriptions, early manuscripts, terminology and the limits of evidence. Chapter 17 keeps inscription evidence apart from later concealment accounts.

https://earlytibet.com/2007/05/06/the-temple-of-the-hat-pillars-and-treasures/

https://earlytibet.com/2008/01/15/early-dzogchen-ii/

https://earlytibet.com/2008/01/24/early-dzogchen-iii/

### 11. Early terma as found manuscripts

Rob Mayer, University of Oxford research blog, 2010. Complete post read. A hypothesis about particular early treasure evidence and manuscript transmission, not a universal explanation of terma or proof of Vimalamitra's authorship.

https://blogs.orient.ox.ac.uk/kila/2010/09/17/early-terma-as-found-manuscripts/

### 12. The origins of the eleven words and meanings

Khenpo Yeshi, Journal of Tibetan Literature 2.2 (2023), pp. 39-82. Introduction, early-history discussion, Nyima Bum account and analytical discussion through the opening of the eleven-topic treatment read. Later translation/comparative sections not fully read. Used only for the earlier systematization, recovered manuscript and argued influence on later authors, not as blanket endorsement of every historical generalization.

https://journaloftibetanliterature.org/index.php/jtl/article/download/69/191/650

### 13. Vima Nyingtik catalogue

Shechen Ontrul Tutob Namgyal, The Maker of Day, translated by Adam Pearcey, Lotsawa House. Introduction, doctrinal survey, publication account and contents inventory read. Fetched ending was truncated. Source for nineteenth-century editorial labor, consultation of older copies, missing texts and supported workers. It is a later catalogue, not a work by Longchenpa or Vimalamitra.

https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/shechen-ontrul-gyurme-tutob-namgyal/vima-nyingtik-karchak

### 14. Zhang Nyibum biography and Essence of the Topics

Lotsawa House. Complete short translations and notes read. The first is translated by Adam Pearcey (2024), its precise authorship uncertain; it provides the lineage portrait of Nyima Bum. The second is Longchen Rabjam, translated by Adam Pearcey (2026), outlining later Heart Essence themes. Technical practice details were not turned into exercises.

https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/longchen-rabjam/zhang-nyibum-biography-vima-nyingtik

https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/longchen-rabjam/essence-topics-zabmo-yangtik

### 15. Sudden and gradual approaches

Joel Gruber, JIABS 39 (2016), pp. 405-427. Publisher abstract and author-shared extract covering pp. 405-417 and the opening of 418 read. Remaining pages were not recovered. Used narrowly for the fact and nature of attribution difficulties, not presented as a full review. Complete manuals not read.

https://poj.peeters-leuven.be/content.php?id=3200532&journal_code=JIABS&url=article

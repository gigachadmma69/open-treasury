# The Great Perfection Is at Play
## Sources, limits and rights notes

Checked 15 November 2026. An eleven-chapter comparative essay about Dzogchen, display and the world's play. This is original commentary with selected short quotations, not a full translation or a reading of any source book. It is not practice transmission, direct introduction, initiation, a deathbed manual or medical advice.

The course keeps Buddhist emptiness and non-self distinct from the divine-play and Shaiva accounts discussed earlier in the series. Its theatre, screen, improvisation and rainbow comparisons are interpretations, not claims that the traditions teach one metaphysics. Traditional accounts of magic, miracles and postmortem experience are identified as religious narratives, not independently verified events. Harm, consent, safety and practical care are not canceled by the language of illusion.

### Primary reading and passage coverage

1. **The All-Creating King, translated by Jim Valby**, in *Ornament of the State of Samantabhadra: Commentary on the All-Creating King*, Kunjed Gyalpo Series, volume one, second edition. Selected passages from root-text chapter 6, One Root, and the volume's commentary on that chapter, not the whole tantra. Chapters 3 and 5 quote 41 words in total from Valby's translation: the passages beginning "Whatever appears is my nature" and "The rolpa energies of the six lokas". Chapter 9 paraphrases the warning about recipients following vehicles of cause and effect and a separate commentary passage about mistaken judgments and attachment. The commentary is not passed off as root tantra. Valby's English is copyrighted; it is not offered under a free license by this course. The quotations are short, credited and directly examined in the surrounding criticism. The complete translation is not reproduced or included in the production package.
   Source record: https://archive.org/details/JimValbyKunjedGyalpoSeriesVol1
   Archive hosting is not evidence of permission or a license. The source's translation and commentary remain their rightsholders' work.

2. **Tibet's Great Yogi Milarepa**, English rendering associated with Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup, edited by W. Y. Evans-Wentz, Oxford University Press, **1928 edition**. Selected passages: ancestral gambling and migration, the later theft of Milarepa's inheritance, the separate feast-house collapse and hailstorm stories, Marpa's towers and the boulder brought "in sport", the hunters, and the Wordless song. The course treats these as hagiography, not documentary proof. The ancestral gambler migrates with his father, not his son. Milarepa is identified as a Kagyu/Mahamudra figure, not relabeled a Dzogchen founder. Modern scholarship identifies Tsangnyon Heruka as the compiler of the celebrated biography, completed in 1488, centuries after Milarepa.
   Actual 1928 scan: https://archive.org/details/dli.ministry.06735
   Text used to check the scan's passages: https://archive.org/download/dli.ministry.06735/11677.E14458_Tibets_Great_Yogi_Milarepa_djvu.txt
   Edition correction: the initially proposed item https://archive.org/details/tibetsgreatyogim0000wyev is a 1971 reprint of the 1951 second edition despite its misleading 1928 metadata. It is not the edition relied on here. No later-edition additions or modern translation are quoted.
   Context: Andrew Quintman's account of the biography's textual history: https://andrewquintman.com/yogin-and-the-madman/

3. **The Tibetan Book of the Dead / Bardo Thodol**, translated by Lama Kazi Dawa-Samdup, edited by W. Y. Evans-Wentz, **1927 edition**. Selected introductory discussion of the three kayas, and translated passages about sounds, apparitions, recognition and the three death-related bardos. This is not a complete account of all bardo teachings. The play/manifestation passage with "Cause of All Causes" is explicitly identified as introductory explanation attributed to Dawa-Samdup, not silently made root text. "Real self" is discussed as this edition's historically loaded English, not proof of an eternal Buddhist Self. Chapter 6 briefly quotes the fearful-visions sentence; its other summaries and theatrical images are commentary. The course does not assert postmortem outcomes as established facts.
   Scan: https://archive.org/details/the-tibetan-book-of-the-dead_202401
   Text: https://archive.org/download/the-tibetan-book-of-the-dead_202401/the%20tibetan%20book%20of%20the%20dead_djvu.txt

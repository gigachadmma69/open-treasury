### Chapter 2 - manuscript and translator

Institutional dates attributed to Cathedral. University's digital-project description supports access and unsettled solutions. Thorpe preface pX, exact fragment 'translations, or rather attempts at translation.' Surrounding apology says versions can be almost or quite as unintelligible as originals, hopes a more competent interpreter will improve them, and notes some poems were left without attempted translation. Script paraphrases this without treating Thorpe as current scholarly authority on every interpretation.

No claim of original composition in 970, no securely named author of the selected riddles, and no invented answer sheet.

### Chapter 3 - moth

Thorpe XLVIII, print p432, PDF page450, manuscript marker [113a]. Exact opening 'A moth ate words' (source terminal semicolon not spoken). Exact ending:
'The stealing guest was not / in aught the wiser, / who the words had swallow'd.'

Both excerpts were compared with OCR and actual rendered page pixels. The intervening line 'and the strong's *' is visibly incomplete/uncertain in this translation and is not silently repaired or quoted. We quote the beginning and ending separately, making no claim they are contiguous or the complete poem.

Number 48 is explicitly Thorpe's, not a claim that modern editions number it 48. Modern ASPR-derived discussion calls it 47. The opening phrase is the stable locator supplied to listeners. 'Bookworm' is a common solution label corroborated by Cavell, not an insect-species determination or literal biological finding.

Interpretation about possession versus understanding is labeled our reading. The course explicitly protects memorization, learning differences and a reader's dignity from the metaphor being weaponized.

Original ethical reasoning: literary ambiguity must not substitute for clear doses, arrangements or consent; no practical medical advice is given. A puzzle participant may request hints/answer/exit. ### Chapter 8 - recognition and return

Original phenomenological invitation, not a neuroscience claim. Acknowledges many quick riddles are valuable merely as quick jokes; the course does not demand hidden profundity in every puzzle. A correct solution can change rather than end a poem's reading.

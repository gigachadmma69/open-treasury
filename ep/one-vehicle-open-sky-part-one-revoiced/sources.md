## Quotation map (script wording → exact source)

### Q1. Ch. 1: "By means of one sole vehicle, the Buddha-vehicle, do I teach creatures the law. There is no second vehicle, nor a third."
Exact (Kern ch. 2): "By means of one sole vehicle, to wit, the Buddha-vehicle, Sâriputra, do I teach creatures the law; there is no second vehicle, nor a third."
Edit for audio: dropped "to wit" and the address to Shariputra. Wording otherwise unchanged.
Source: https://sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot02.htm — public domain.

### Q2. Ch. 2: "None but a Tathagata can impart to a Tathagata those laws which the Tathagata knows."
Exact (Kern ch. 2): identical sentence (with "Sâriputra" address around it). Unchanged.
Source: same URL. PD.

### Q3. Ch. 2 scene: meditation, earth shakes, flowers, light-ray; Maitreya asks Manjushri.
Source: Kern ch. 1 (https://sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot01.htm). The ray issues "from within the circle of hair between the eyebrows"; it lights thousands of Buddha-fields (prose: "eighteen hundred thousand"; Maitreya's stanza: "eighteen thousand"; script deliberately says "thousands of Buddha-fields at once" to avoid the variant). Paraphrase, not quotation. PD.

### Q4. Ch. 2: Shariputra asks three times; Buddha declines twice; five thousand walk out; the Buddha's silence shows assent.
Exact anchor (Kern ch. 2): "Now it happened that the five thousand proud monks, nuns and lay devotees of both sexes in the congregation rose from their seats and, after saluting with their heads the Lord's feet, went to leave the assembly... Therefore, thinking themselves aggrieved, they went to leave the assembly, to which the Lord by his silence showed assent." Script paraphrases closely; quote of the walkout is faithful. PD.
Note: source text has typos "congregatino" (OCR); not reproduced.

### Q5. Ch. 3: four verbs — show, open, awaken, lead.
Exact (Kern ch. 2): "To show all creatures the sight of Tathâgata-knowledge does the Buddha... appear in the world; to open the eyes of creatures for the sight of Tathâgata-knowledge...; with the awakening (or admonishing) by the display of Tathâgata-knowledge; with leading the teaching of Tathâgata-knowledge on the right path." Script compresses to the four verbs. PD.

### Q6. Ch. 3: "There is but one vehicle, Shariputra, and that the Buddha-vehicle."
Exact (Kern ch. 2): "There is but one vehicle, Sâriputra, and that the Buddha-vehicle." Diacritics respelled only. PD.

### Q7. Ch. 3: "have all of them reached supreme, perfect enlightenment."
Exact (Kern ch. 2): "...who have heard the law from the past Tathâgatas, &c., have all of them reached supreme, perfect enlightenment." "&c." dropped for audio. PD.

### Q8. Ch. 4: burning house parable.
Retold closely from Kern ch. 3 (https://sacred-texts.com/bud/lotus/lot03.htm), including: one door ("The house had but one door"); children unaware ("do not perceive, nor know, nor understand... the word 'burning'"); father's expedient with "bullock-carts, goat-carts, deer-carts"; children rush out "with the cry of 'Who shall arrive first, the very first?'"; father "goes and sits down in the open air on the square of the village, his heart filled with joy"; all children receive identical great carts "made of seven precious substances... yoked with white, very fair and fleet bullocks... swift as the wind"; the falsehood question ("Has that man made himself guilty of a falsehood...?") and Shariputra's verdict ("By no means, Lord... Even in this case... the man would not have been guilty of falsehood"); the identification of house = triple world, father = Tathagata, three carts = three vehicles, one cart = one Buddha-vehicle. Script compresses but changes no element. PD.

### Q9. Ch. 5: "use, skilfully, to designate that one and sole Buddha-vehicle by the appellation of the threefold vehicle."
Exact (Kern ch. 2): identical (context: at the decay of the epoch). PD.

### Q10. Ch. 6: Garab Dorje biography.
Source: Longchen Rabjam, "Symbolic Transmission of the Vidyādharas" from the Jewel Rosary history, Lotsawa House translation: https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/longchen-rabjam/symbolic-vidyadharas-from-jewel-rosary (CC BY-NC 4.0). Details used: nun Sudharmā, daughter of King Uparāja and Queen Ālokabhāsvatī in Oḍḍiyāna; dream of white crystalline man placing vase on her head; son born ten months later; hidden "in a pit of ash"; ḍākinīs/devas/nāgas descend with offerings; named Vetālabhava; at seven, debates and defeats five hundred paṇḍitas of the royal court; king names him Prahevajra; Mañjuśrīmitra meets him at Śītavana; "Garab Dorje then dissolved into a mass of light"; Mañjuśrīmitra's cry ("If the light of the teacher's lamp should fade, who will dispel the darkness of the world?"); "a precious casket, the size of a fingernail, which contained the testament known as The Three Statements that Strike the Vital Point, fell from the sky." Script paraphrases with one direct quote of the cry (compressed). All labeled "the tradition tells it" in the script. CC BY-NC 4.0: attributed in episode notes; no dana button.
Name glosses: "Prahevajra (Supremely Joyful Vajra)" is the translation's own gloss; "Rolang Dewa, the Blissful Revenant" from Pearcey's note 2 on the Three Statements page (same license). "Samantabhadra → Vajrasattva → Garab Dorje" transmission framing: standard tradition claim, labeled as tradition (also on the same Lotsawa pages).

### Q11. Ch. 6: Three Statements quotations.
Source: "Striking the Vital Point in Three Statements," tr. Adam Pearcey, Lotsawa House 2025: https://www.lotsawahouse.org/indian-masters/garab-dorje/three-statements-that-strike-vital-point (CC BY-NC 4.0).
Quoted lines, exact: "This awareness, which has no concrete identity, Is totally unconfined in its modes of manifestation. All that appears and exists thus arises as the dharmakāya field, And this very arising is liberated directly upon itself." — and — "Introducing directly the face of rigpa itself. Deciding upon one thing and one thing only. Confidence directly in the liberation of rising thoughts." Read verbatim (diacritics respelled for audio). Snake image: "just as the snake itself uncoils its own knots" (exact) embedded in original commentary. Attribution in notes; no dana button.

### Q12. Ch. 8: sudden/gradual in China; Chan mirror verses; Samye debate c. late 8th century (Moheyan vs Kamalashila); callback to our episode "The Argument That Would Not End".
Labeled in the script as the standard scholarly picture and "story" (Platform Sutra poetry contest is presented as the tradition's "greatest story", not as fact). Samye dating "around the end of the eighth century" is the conventional scholarly framing. Sources for the audience: our own episode (its ledger). No quotations. Original commentary.

### Q13. Ch. 2 addition: udumbara rarity + beyond reasoning.
Exact (Kern ch. 2): "It is but now and then, Sâriputra, that the Tathâgata preaches such a discourse on the law as this. just as but now and then is seen the blossom of the glomerous fig-tree, Sâriputra..." and "It is not by reasoning, Sâriputra, that the law is to be found: it is beyond the pale of reasoning, and must be learnt from the Tathâgata." Script compresses; faithful. PD. (Udumbara gloss "a flower said to appear once in an age" is the standard traditional gloss, labeled as such.)

### Q14. Ch. 4 opening: Shariputra's confession and ecstasy.
Exact anchor (Kern ch. 3): "Whereas the entrance into the fixed points of the law is nominally equal, we have been dismissed by the Lord with the inferior vehicle... because, without understanding the mystery of the Lord... we heard only in a hurry... therefore, O Lord, I used to pass day and night in self-reproach... My burning has left me, O Lord, now that I have heard this wonderful law..." Script compresses to his mountain-cave confession and the exact line "My burning has left me." PD.

### Q15. Ch. 4: one-door reflection + the father's generosity.
Exact anchors (Kern ch. 3): "This house has but one opening; the door is shut; and those boys, fickle, unsteady, and childlike as they are, will, it is to be feared, run hither and thither, and come to grief and disaster in this mass of fire." and "Why should I give these boys inferior carts, all these boys being my own children, dear and precious?... I could give such great vehicles to all beings, how much more then to my own children." Script paraphrases both faithfully; "a lesser nirvana... ungenerous" is commentary, phrased as the text's argument, which it is. PD.

### Q16. Ch. 5: no falsehood.
Exact (Kern ch. 2): "for there is no falsehood in the Tathâgatas, Sâriputra. There is but one vehicle, Sâriputra, and that the Buddha-vehicle." Script compresses. PD.

### Q17. Ch. 6: Three Statements mother/child + confidence like space.
Exact (Pearcey/Lotsawa): "Whatever appears is one's own nature; There is greater power in identity — Like recognizing like. The mother recognizes the mother itself. The child recognizes itself... Awareness alone connects to itself" and "To have mastery of the site of freedom brings confidence like space into which space dissolves." Script quotes with light ellipsis and respelling; attributed; CC BY-NC 4.0; no dana button stands.

## Claims check on script lines
- "maybe twenty-four centuries ago... written down a few hundred years later": the Buddha's parinirvana is conventionally dated c. 5th century BCE; the Lotus Sutra was composed in writing centuries later (standard scholarly view). Script hedges with "according to the tradition" and "maybe". OK.
- "northeast India": Rajagriha/Vulture Peak is in Bihar. OK.
- "the dragon girl we will meet later" (Lotus ch. 12): forward reference, verified in Kern TOC (ch. 12 = Devadatta chapter containing the dragon girl). OK.
- "The dzogchen tradition itself says the view without the grounding is worse than no view": presented as a tradition-wide characterization, not a quotation. Commonly attested sentiment (e.g., warnings in the tantras and commentaries). Kept general; no specific citation claimed. OK.
- No personal details about Joe anywhere. OK.

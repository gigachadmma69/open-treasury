# No One at the Center
## Vasubandhu and the World That Mind Makes
### Source companion | 18 November 2026

Original commentary for The Open Treasury / Narrated by Tara. This is a route through Vasubandhu and Yogacara, not a complete translation, a reading of every attributed work, or a replacement for the source texts.

## The question to keep

How can continuity, responsibility and liberation remain intelligible without a permanent owner behind experience?

The course moves from the Abhidharma world and the Treasury through Yogacara's accounts of consciousness, conditioning and the three natures. It then tests the arguments about external objects, other minds and realization. The opening phone-message scene and all modern examples are original illustrations, not historical incidents or translations.

## Terms without a warehouse

**Abhidharma:** Buddhist analysis and systematization of experience, causation and the path. The Treasury presents a particular scholastic world and also debates it.

**Dharma:** context-sensitive. Here often a factor or phenomenon under analysis, not always the Buddha's teaching as a whole.

**Five aggregates:** form, feeling, perception/recognition, formations and consciousness. These are analytical groupings, not five little persons.

**Alaya-vijnana:** foundational or store consciousness. A conditioned stream used to explain continuity, maturation and dispositions. Not an immortal self, universal mind or repository full of literal objects.

**Manas / afflicted mentation:** in the eight-consciousness account, the appropriating function that mistakes the foundational stream for self. The same Sanskrit term can have different uses in other contexts.

**Bija / seed:** a causal capacity or disposition, not a tiny durable item stored inside a container.

**Vijnapti-matra:** rendered in different settings as representation-only, cognition-only, appearance-only and awareness-only. These choices carry interpretive weight; no one English phrase settles the metaphysics.

**Three natures:** imagined/constructed, dependent and perfected. The perfected is explained as the absence of the imagined in the dependent. It is not an extra substance behind the other two.

**Ashraya-paravritti:** transformation of the basis. The course explains a path-level claim, not a result that this audio promises to confer.

**Akara, sakara, nirakara:** aspect/form/image, with-aspect and without-aspect. The vocabulary and classifications vary. Later debates cannot simply be projected backward onto all earlier thinkers.

## Claims and boundaries

### Biography
Fourth or fifth century CE is a cautious working placement. Traditional accounts disagree. Conversion stories are identified as stories. Scholarly disputes about the unity and attribution of the corpus are acknowledged rather than resolved by fiat. No precise birth/death dates or claim that all attributed works are authentic.

### Three natures
The text-message example introduces the relation between constructed appearance, conditioning and absence of a falsely imputed structure. It is not the whole doctrine. Yogacara reaches the grasping subject-object division, not only occasional mistaken judgments. Different scholars disagree about the ultimate status of the dependent process.

### Twenty Verses
The early replies show that the opponent's alleged impossibility is not established on the debate's premises. They are not all positive proofs of idealism. Dream examples do not demonstrate that waking life is a dream. Hungry ghosts and hell examples depend on Buddhist cosmological assumptions, not independent modern evidence.

### Atoms
The target is ancient theories of partless atoms and wholes. No claim about modern particle physics or quantum confirmation. Defeating a specific ancient account would not automatically defeat every possible realism.

### Other minds
Vasubandhu accepts other streams and their influence. This intention does not by itself solve all tensions in the argument. Dharmakirti's inference and Ratnakirti's later challenge are identified as later developments. No claim that one personal mind is all that exists.

### Practice and ethics
The exercise is original, mild and optional. It is not an initiation, a traditional guided practice, psychotherapy or a promise of realization. Stop if unsettled. No-self does not erase persons, consent, boundaries, obligations or injury. Karma is not evidence that a sufferer deserved what happened.

### Modern comparisons
Learning, habit and the phone-message scene help explain a concept. They do not establish that Yogacara is neuroscience, predictive processing, simulation theory or modern idealism under another name. The course's causal comparisons do not replace the tradition's rebirth and soteriological commitments.

## Sources and reading route

### 1. Jonathan C. Gold, "Vasubandhu," Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vasubandhu/

Main scholarly map for biography, the Treasury's arguments, interpretation, appearance-only and the three natures. The argument sections used here and the opening of Gold's idealism discussion were read, together with the biography and works overview. Gold offers an interpretation, not a neutral final verdict that overrides every rival reading. Best starting point for the course's central question about causal explanation.

### 2. Szilvia Szanyi, "Yogacara," Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 2024
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/yogacara/

Main broad map for layered textual history, alaya and manas, idealism, the three natures, later epistemologists and other minds. The relevant sections on history, philosophical psychology, idealism and other minds were read. Useful beside Gold because the wider tradition is not reducible to a single author's system.

### 3. Vasubandhu, The Thirty Verses, translated by David Karma Choephel, Dharma Ebooks, January 2022
https://dharmaebooks.org/the-thirty-verses/

The complete publisher PDF was obtained and all 30 verses, notes, colophon and license were read. This is the primary backbone for the eight-consciousness account, three natures and warning against mistaking a mind-only thought for realization. License: CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; English translation copyright Khenpo David Karma Choephel. Used for research, not reproduced or narrated here. No claim to have made an independent Sanskrit or Tibetan translation.

### 4. Vasubandhu, Twenty Verses with Auto-Commentary, teaching translation hosted by Nilanjan Das
https://www.dasnilanjan.com/uploads/5/7/6/4/57640813/vasubandhu_twenty_verses.pdf

The returned teaching selection was read in full. It contains gaps and ellipses, including a jump in verse numbering; this is not a reading of the entire auto-commentary. Used with the scholarly accounts for the objections, dream replies, atom arguments, other minds and closing epistemic limits. The conventional title "Twenty" need not match a simple count of numbered stanzas in every edition. No translation text is reproduced in the narration.

### 5. Matthew MacKenzie, "The Yogacara Theory of Three Natures: Internalist and Non-Dualist Interpretations," Comparative Philosophy 9.1, 2018
https://scholarworks.sjsu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1240&context=comparativephilosophy

Main interpretive sections and conclusion read. Supports the contrast between internalist and nondual reconstructions and the importance of intersubjectivity. Its preference for a nondual reconstruction is identified as an argued position, not consensus or proof that idealist readings are incompetent.

### 6. William S. Waldron, "On Selves and Selfless Discourse," 2006, author-hosted paper
https://www.middlebury.edu/college/sites/default/files/2023-03/waldron-on_selves_and_selfless_discourse0.pdf

The relevant discussion of causal analysis, subtle consciousness, language, shared worlds and the indispensable context of personal discourse was read. Supports chapter 12's attention to intersubjective conditioning and the course's refusal to turn impersonal analysis into indifference to persons. The entire comparative grammatical argument is not presented as covered.

### 7. Yuichi Kajiyama, "Controversy between the sakara- and nirakara-vadins of the Yogacara school: some materials," Journal of Indian and Buddhist Studies 14.1
https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ibk1952/14/1/14_1_429/_pdf

Relevant opening and classification discussion read, including the caveats about aligning Chinese, Sanskrit and Tibetan reports and the later development of the controversy. Supports the conceptual doorway in chapter 20, not a complete history of the debate. Original scan/OCR has spelling and extraction defects; no OCR wording is quoted.

### 8. Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Vasubandhu"
https://iep.utm.edu/vasubandhu/

The biographical, works and reception account was read. Corroborates the breadth of the corpus and East Asian influence. Some categorical biographical claims are treated more cautiously beside Gold, including its dismissal of the two-author hypothesis. The course does not adopt its exact counts of works or precise dates.

### 9. Khenpo Shenpen Nangwa, Life of Vasubandhu, translated by Adam Pearcey, Lotsawa House, 2020
https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/khenpo-shenga/life-of-vasubandhu

Complete short traditional account read. Used as evidence of how a later Tibetan community remembered the figure, not independent confirmation of supernatural episodes, debated chronology or large numerical claims. No translation is reproduced.

### 10. Vasubandhu, selected quotations from the Treasury of Abhidharma, translated by Adam Pearcey, Lotsawa House
https://www.lotsawahouse.org/indian-masters/vasubandhu/treasury-abhidharma-quotations

Complete short selection read. Corroborates the centrality of karma, affliction, discipline, learning and practice. It is a selection, not the complete Treasury. No translation is reproduced.

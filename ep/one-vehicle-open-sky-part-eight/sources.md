# Part Eight: One Vehicle, One Taste, source and accuracy ledger
22 November 2026. This is a comparative interpretation, not a claim that the Lotus and Great Perfection are doctrinally identical. It introduces no new scripture into the course, but rechecks the quotations against the published texts named below.

## Eight direct quotations checked
The frozen script has eight straight-quoted passages, all matched against the staged sources with zero misses. Case, punctuation, verse whitespace, diacritics and numerical footnote markers are normalized for matching. Shariputra is the spoken form used by the course for Kern's Sariputra. A match validates wording under those normalizations, not the interpretation.

## Public-domain Lotus translation
H. Kern, The Saddharma-Pundarika, or The Lotus of the True Law, Sacred Books of the East XXI (Oxford, 1884). Public domain.
- Chapter II, Skilfulness: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Sacred_Books_of_the_East/Volume_21/Chapter_2 . Used for the one sole vehicle sentence (chapters 2 and 10), and the shorter one-vehicle sentence (chapter 2). Also consulted for the Buddha's account of teaching adapted to dispositions and the statement that the teaching must be learned from the Tathagata, discussed in chapter 6.
- Chapter III, A Parable: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Sacred_Books_of_the_East/Volume_21/Chapter_3 . Used for the two passages in chapter 9: the triple world as the Buddha's domain, and the father's happiness after rescuing his children. The course does not use these to deny suffering or the importance of rescue.
- Lifespan verse, Kern chapter XV, Kumarajiva chapter 16: the world perceived as burning while the Buddhafield teems with gods and men, quoted in chapter 4. Reused from the text staged and read for Parts 5 and 6; consult their ledgers for the edition history. The course does not equate that verse with every claim about primordial awareness.

## Adam Pearcey translations, Lotsawa House, CC BY-NC 4.0
- Garab Dorje, Striking the Vital Point in Three Statements (2025): https://www.lotsawahouse.org/indian-masters/garab-dorje/three-statements-that-strike-vital-point . Quoted for the parting-advice description in chapter 6 and the three central statements in chapter 10. Its presentation as a final testament and the account of the grieving disciple are reported as the text's lineage narrative, not independent historical evidence. Its language of a path and introduction corrects the overly simple contrast between a sutra path and a supposedly pathless tradition.
- Mipham, A Dzogchen Instruction (2026): https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/mipham/dzogchen-instruction . Its aspiration for one's own and others' welfare is discussed in chapter 8.
- Mipham, The Vajra Mirror (2016): https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/mipham/vajra-mirror-self-awareness . Discussed as an argument about self-awareness and Madhyamaka, not cited as a direct treatment of abuse or a historical explanation for secrecy.
- Longchen Rabjam, The Heart of Definitive Meaning (2025): https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/longchen-rabjam/heart-definitive-zabmo-yangtik . Space-and-appearance image paraphrased in chapters 2, 4 and 9.
- The Tantra of Self-Emergent Awareness (2025): https://www.lotsawahouse.org/tibetan-masters/pema-ledrel-tsal/self-emergent-awareness-tantra . Related-cycle text paraphrased as part of the chorus from Part Seven, not represented as one of the Seventeen.

## Limits
This is discussion of view, not practice instruction, empowerment, transmission or a substitute for a qualified teacher. It does not establish the experiential claims of its sources. It does not certify any community or teacher. It does not present all restrictions as identical or derive a single historical reason for them. The Seventeen Tantras remain unread by this course; a complete translation has neither been made nor established as a scheduled undertaking. The road-and-ground metaphor is an interpretive proposal, not proof of common origin or identical doctrine. Ethical limits in chapters 7 and 8 are the course's own explicit commitments.

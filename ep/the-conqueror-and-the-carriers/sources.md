# The Conqueror and the Carriers
## Mao, Tibet, and the Teachings That Survived

### Source and scope notes

An original historical essay about Mao's political and military effectiveness, the human costs of his rule, religious destruction in Tibetan regions, and the work of people who preserved and shared Tibetan teachings. It separates causal consequences from moral credit. It does not treat the global teaching diaspora as a justification for persecution.

This is a selective synthesis of the sources below, not a claim to have read every book mentioned by those sources. Some material was available only as an extract, review, public research description, or institutional account. Those limits are identified where they affect the argument.

### Military and political method

- Mao Zedong, [On Protracted War, May 1938](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-2/mswv2%5F09.htm). The episode uses the opening argument about relative strength, time, international conditions and the united front. A primary political text, not independent proof of the author's account of his own success.
- Adam Elkus, [Strategy and the Chinese Civil War](https://www.militarystrategymagazine.com/article/strategy-and-the-chinese-civil-war/), Military Strategy Magazine, 2012. A military-centered interpretation, useful for the importance of organization, conventional campaigns, Nationalist operational failures, and collective rather than single-leader agency.
- Jill S. Russell, [review of Odd Arne Westad's Decisive Encounters](https://www.h-net.org/reviews/showrev.php?id=8510), H-War, 2003. The episode explicitly draws on Russell's review, not a claimed complete reading of Westad's book.
- Mao Zedong, [Some Questions Concerning Methods of Leadership, June 1, 1943](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-3/mswv3_13.htm). Primary source for the mass line, particular investigation, leadership groups, and task priorities.
- Frederick C. Teiwes, [The Origins of Rectification: Inner-Party Purges and Education before Liberation](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/china-quarterly/article/abs/origins-of-rectification-innerparty-purges-and-education-before-liberation/CD0DC33AA6D8360D606EF1B41DD613B0), The China Quarterly. The publicly available extract and reference notes, not the complete article, support the discussion of the persuasive-coercive continuum and the limits of a simple Mao-versus-other-leaders contrast.

### Famine and Cultural Revolution violence

- Felix Wemheuer, [Great Leap Forward and the Famine](https://www.oxfordbibliographies.com/display/document/obo-9780199920082/obo-9780199920082-0129.xml), Oxford Bibliographies, 2016. Introduction and overview. Its 15-45 million range describes the spread of published famine estimates, not an agreed count made with identical definitions.
- Zhongwei Zhao and Anna Reimondos, [The Demography of China's 1958-61 Famine: A Closer Examination](https://shs.cairn.info/journal-population-2012-2-page-281?lang=en), Population 67(2), 2012. Consulted sections include the introduction, survey methods and limitations, and marriage, fertility and mortality analysis. The episode distinguishes excess deaths from missing births, and does not portray this paper as a new independent proof of a 30 million nationwide total.
- Cormac Ó Gráda, [Great Leap, Great Famine](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/rest/bitstreams/10261/retrieve), UCD working paper WP13/04, 2013. A critical review of major famine accounts, including numerical methods, regional differences, Lushan, political pressure and failed correction. Yang Jisheng's Tombstone and Zhou Xun's documentary collection are discussed through this review, not presented as books independently read in full for this episode.
- Andrew G. Walder, interviewed by Melissa De Witte, [How violence unfolded during China's Cultural Revolution](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2019/10/violence-unfolded-chinas-cultural-revolution), Stanford Report, 2019. The estimate discussed is tied to this research and its scope. It is not a census of all causes of death over the entire 1966-76 period.
- Andrew G. Walder, [Rebellion and Repression in China, 1966-1971](https://sociology.stanford.edu/sites/sociology/files/walder-ssh.pdf). Abstract and relevant methods, event-coding and timing sections were used to check the distinction between reported local deaths and extrapolated totals. Earlier and later Walder studies differ in coverage.

### Tibetan regions, destruction and survival

- Melvyn C. Goldstein, [Tibet and China in the Twentieth Century](https://uw.manifoldapp.org/read/57ea43bb-1b0e-46b6-8ed2-67fd3d9f84a7/section/2ad9a972-e42d-4f98-8946-23a88c43a247), in Governing China's Multiethnic Frontiers. Relevant historical and policy sections. The distinctions between Lhasa-administered Tibet, eastern Tibetan regions, and the later Tibet Autonomous Region are essential. Goldstein's interpretation is not treated as the only Tibetan historical perspective.
- Mao Zedong, [marginal note of February 18, 1959](https://digitalarchive.umd.edu/document/94423/download), Wilson Center / University of Maryland Digital Archive, Chinese transcript and English translation by David Wolff. The episode paraphrases the note's treatment of disorder as an opportunity. This short document does not establish that Mao orchestrated every event of the subsequent uprising.
- [Chronology of Events](https://dalailama.com/the-dalai-lama/events-and-awards/chronology-of-events), Office of the Dalai Lama. A direct source for the exile position that the 1951 agreement was signed under duress and later repudiated, not an independent resolution of the dispute.
- Teresa Poole, [Lost Tibetan text exposes Chinese brutality](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/lost-tibetan-text-exposes-chinese-brutality-1144281.html), The Independent, February 12, 1998. Contemporary reporting on publication of the Panchen Lama's 1962 petition as A Poisoned Arrow. The original petition was not independently read in full for this episode. No precise monastery-loss or Tibetan population-loss total is asserted from the newspaper summary.
- Tsering Shakya, [Tibetan Questions](https://newleftreview.org/issues/ii51/articles/tsering-shakya-tibetan-questions), New Left Review 51, 2008. Available opening interview sections concerning economic deterioration, reform, religious revival and Tibetan debates. His book The Dragon in the Land of Snows is relevant further reading, not a book claimed read in full here.
- Dáša Pejchar Mortensen, [research description on Tibetan participation in the Cultural Revolution in Gyelthang](https://weai.columbia.edu/events/disruptive-narratives-memory-and-legacy-tibetan-participation-cultural-revolution-gyelthang), Columbia Weatherhead East Asian Institute, 2023. A public description of a particular oral-history study, not the complete lecture or a finding about all Tibetan communities.
- [Introduction to Sera Monastery](https://texts.mandala.library.virginia.edu/book_pubreader/15983), University of Virginia Mandala / Tibetan and Himalayan Library. Relevant essay sections on the 1970 reestablishment at Bylakuppe and restoration in Tibet during the 1980s. Population numbers in this older account are not used as current figures.

### Transmission and adaptation

- Peter Oldmeadow, [Globalisation and Tibetan Buddhism](https://openjournals.library.sydney.edu.au/SSR/article/download/233/212), Sydney Studies in Religion. Relevant historical, institutional and adaptation sections. A distinction between Tibetan continuity and Western forms, rather than a claim that Western visibility measures Tibetan survival.
- [Chogyam Trungpa biography](https://chogyamtrungpa.com/about/chogyam-trungpa-biography/), Chogyam Trungpa Institute. An institutional source for one route through India, Britain and North America. It is not an independent assessment of the teacher's ethics, and influence is not treated as an ethical endorsement.
- [Saint Petersburg Buddhist temple centenary exhibition](https://spbmuseum.ru/exhibits_and_exhibitions/temporary_exhibitions/48012/), State Museum of the History of Saint Petersburg, 2015, Russian. The temple's construction and 1915 consecration provide a concrete counterexample to the idea that Tibetan Buddhism first reached outside Tibet after 1959. No claim that it was the first Buddhist temple in Europe is adopted.

### The two attributed predictions

These have different evidence trails. They should not be merged into one authenticated early text.

**Iron bird, wheeled horses and scattered ants:** The combined saying appears in an actual [New York Times letter of October 31, 1976](https://www.nytimes.com/1976/10/31/archives/letters-do-unto-others-twain-meets-earful-consciousness-raising.html). This establishes circulation in English by that date. It does not establish the earliest occurrence, an eighth-century date, or a securely identified Tibetan passage. A newspaper letter's attribution is not independent authentication.

Sam van Schaik's [The red-faced men I: Warriors with painted faces](https://earlytibet.com/2007/09/18/red-faced-men/), 2007, discusses the missing reliable source and older Tibetan uses of red-faced descriptions. Leads for an earlier, different red-faced-country prediction do not by themselves date the iron-bird composite.

**Dark age and wildfire:** The wording has a verified modern teaching context in Natural Dharma Fellowship's [Igniting the Flame of Wisdom retreat description](https://naturaldharma.org/resource/2022-06-igniting-the-flame-of-wisdom/), alongside Advice from the Lotus-Born. The [publisher's book description](https://www.rangjung.com/book_title/advice-from-the-lotus-born-2/) and [Rangjung Yeshe reference entry](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/Advice_from_the_Lotus-Born) establish the collection's identity, translation and introductory teaching context. They do not, in the material consulted, identify an older Tibetan source for that particular line. No claim of a verified prediction of Mao or modern technology follows from those pages.

Not finding an earlier source is not proof of fabrication. Traditional attribution, revelation history, manuscript date, translation date and modern interpretation are different questions.

### Rights and limits

The narration and commentary are original. Any CC0 dedication applies only to that original material, not to the books, translations, newspaper letters, photographs or other sources cited.

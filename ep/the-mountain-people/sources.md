# The Mountain People: source ledger

Research and final check: 15 November 2026. Narrative is an original synthesis, not a modern translation reading. Historical assertions, traditional claims, and editorial interpretations are separated in the spoken text. This is a source-backed introductory course, not an academic critical edition. Some biography details rely on general reference sources and should not be promoted into archival certainties.

## Primary texts read

1. Zhuangzi, chapter 1, Chinese text with James Legge's historical translation, Chinese Text Project: https://ctext.org/zhuangzi/enjoyment-in-untroubled-ease . Chapter 2 draws on the Gu Ye/Gushe spirit-person passage and the reported doubt about it. The descriptive sentences adapt Legge's wording; this is a public-domain historical translation, not a quotation from a recent copyrighted edition. The reading as an image of freedom is explicitly interpretive, not a claim that all ancient readers rejected bodily immortality.
2. Lie Xian Zhuan, Peng Zu entry, Chinese Text Project: https://ctext.org/lie-xian-zhuan/peng-zu . Chapter 3 uses the original Chinese: over 800 years, fine zhi, daoyin/xingqi, shrine, rain prayers, two tigers, ascent. English narration is a short original paraphrase of the Chinese. The site's displayed AI/user English is not treated as a critical translation. Collection authorship and dates are not presented as secure.

## Specialist scholarship read directly

3. Fabrizio Pregadio, "Zhang Boduan and His Work": https://www.goldenelixir.com/jindan/neidan_and_wzp_1.html . Chapters 9-10: biographical uncertainty, poetic structure, wide circulation, the retrospective formation of Nanzong, no direct Zhang-to-Shi Tai discipleship, later integration with Quanzhen, retrospective identification of the anonymous Sichuan teacher with Liu Haichan and links to Zhongli/Lü. Paraphrase only, no modern translation excerpts.
4. Pregadio, "Doctrinal Sources": https://goldenelixir.com/jindan/neidan_and_wzp_2.html . Chapters 9-10: neidan is diverse, not one school; spiritual and practice-oriented interpretations; Daoist and Buddhist sources; allusion as doctrinal signaling; Cantong qi traditionally second-century but received text written/completed centuries later. This source takes precedence over the overconfident 142 CE date in general reference accounts. No claim to a securely dated "first alchemy book in the world."
5. Stanley Henning, "Ignorance, Legend and Taijiquan," Journal of the Chen Style Taijiquan Research Association of Hawaii 2(3), 1994, pp. 1-7, complete text independently checked: https://crnagorataiji.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/legend-in-tai-chi.pdf . Chapter 12: three stages of the Zhang legend (immortal; after 1669 internal boxing; explicit tai chi attribution after 1900), Huang Zongxi epitaph, uncertainty of historical Zhang. Henning's framework is attributed, not made a universal scholarly verdict. Paraphrase only.

## General reference and folklore sources read

8. Xian: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xian_(Taoism) . Character variants, Shuowen and Shiming definitions, semantic range, Ge Hong's three ranks, corpse-liberation substitutes. Such taxonomies are religious classifications, not observed physiology.
9. Ge Hong: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ge_Hong . Family, great-uncle Ge Xuan (not grandfather), military/official career, Baopuzi c.317, southward request for cinnabar, Mount Luofu, competing death dates 343/364. No verified escape from death claimed.
10. Xu Fu: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Fu . 219/210 BCE expeditions, spectacular fleet descriptions, non-return, later Japan traditions and the late date of their attestations. The voyage narrative does not verify the elixir, exact crew totals, or destination.
11. Chinese alchemical elixir poisoning: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_alchemical_elixir_poisoning . Toxic ingredients and historical poisoning accounts. Qin Shi Huang's mercury death is explicitly a hypothesis, not a forensic finding. Not all elixirs were identical, nor did external alchemy simply stop when neidan emerged.
12. Lü Dongbin: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BC_Dongbin . Traditional biography, conflicting examination stories, Yellow Millet Dream, ten trials, White Peony story, sword iconography, Golden Flower attribution, extraordinary 220-year claim. All miracle episodes are treated as legends. Traditional 796 birth is not a validated modern birth record.
13. Eight Immortals: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Immortals . Roster, emblems, gender-variable Lan Caihe, Ming novels, earlier art, sea crossing and proverb, Xi'an temple/wine-shop legend. Unnecessary largest/founding-date claims are omitted. The sea-crossing suggestion is correctly attributed to Lü as choosing distinctive powers rather than clouds. No claim that a wine shop has been archaeologically verified beneath the temple.
14. E. T. C. Werner, Myths and Legends of China (1922), historical folklore collection, book record https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15250 and text pages read https://www.bookrags.com/ebooks/15250/156.html and https://www.bookrags.com/ebooks/15250/159.html . Li Tieguai's borrowed body and Zhang Guolao's white mule. Historical folklore, not independent evidence that either miracle happened. No modern wording copied from these pages.
15. Queen Mother of the West: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Mother_of_the_West . Chapter 7: Kunlun, fearsome early representation, later court, peaches and variant ripening cycles, King Mu and Han Wudi legends. Interpretations about kingship and access are the course's analysis. No claim of actual immortality-conferring fruit.
16. Qiu Chuji: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiu_Chuji . 1148-1227, journey begun 1220, camp meeting 1222, no-elixir response, tax exemption 1223, Li Zhichang travel account, fewer desires/less killing counsel. Course paraphrases the answer, rather than claiming to read an original Mongol transcript. Actual effect on massacre totals is left unestablished.
17. Neidan: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neidan and Wuzhen pian: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuzhen_pian . Vocabulary jing/qi/shen, immortal embryo, Wuzhen preface 1075, traditional 1069 teaching encounter. Specialist source #4 overrides the overly certain Cantong qi date. No embodied technique instructions.
18. Zhang Sanfeng: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Sanfeng . Legendary 212 years, titles, Wudang associations, 1669 epitaph, Chen Wangting attribution, Jin Yong novel. Historical Zhang and precise tai chi origins remain subjects of research; a legendary founder is not necessary to validate modern practice.
19. Yangsheng: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangsheng_(Daoism) . Mawangdui tomb sealed 168 BCE, daoyin and breath-cultivation context. The unsupported spoken comparative dating of the inscription is omitted. Antiquity of bodily cultivation is not proof of an unbroken lineage for modern tai chi.

## Boundaries and editorial judgments

- No verified human immortality or validated 200/800-year lifespan is established by these sources.
- Health-outcome research on modern exercise does not authenticate an ancient biography or alchemical mechanism. No medical prescription, survival guarantee, fasting/breath-retention instructions, sexual method, ingestion recipe, or teacher-restricted exercise is given.
- The story opening does not collapse early poetry and later religion into a proven simple evolution; multiple readings and overlapping practices existed.
- No present-life teacher endorsement is implied by a lineage diagram. Ethical conduct and competence need their own judgment.

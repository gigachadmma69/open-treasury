# Lila: The Play That Makes Worlds
## Sources, editions, rights and production notes

An original ten-chapter comparative course anchored in Vedanta, with Plato, Heraclitus, Krishna devotion, Coomaraswamy, Schiller and Nietzsche. Selected passages were checked, not the complete bodies of work. This is not a comprehensive history, a translation of a whole book, a practice transmission or evidence that these traditions teach one doctrine. Comparative images and our ethical synthesis are distinguished from source claims.

## Editorial boundaries

The final production text removes an inaccurate generation-gap claim about Heraclitus and Plato, an unreliable Sanskrit word-count claim, sweeping unanimity claims, an unsourced claim about every major museum, and suggestions that the Nataraja image predicted modern physics. The relation between the child at the board and two interpretive standpoints is our reading, not a literal historical doctrine attributed to Heraclitus. The metaphor about ignorance being restrained is labeled as ours. Animal-play scenes are illustrations; they do not establish a scientific theory of why animals play.

The callback to The Safeword Is Recognition refers to the earlier released episode. It does not claim that recognition cancels illness, grief or ordinary consequences. No one may use cosmic play as a license for coercion or for dismissing another person's pain.

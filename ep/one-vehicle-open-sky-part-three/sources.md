Chapters used: III (A Parable; prediction to Shariputra), IV (Willingness; the prodigal son), VI (Announcement of Future Destiny; the four predictions), VIII (Announcement; the five hundred and the jewel in the robe), XIX (Sadaparibhuta). ## Quotes, chapter by chapter

### Chapter 2 (script) from Kern ch. III

1. "Again, Sâriputra, at a future period, after innumerable, inconceivable, immeasurable Æons, when thou shalt have learnt the true law of hundred thousand myriads of kotis of Tathâgatas, showed devotion in various ways, and achieved the present Bodhisattva-course, thou shalt become in the world a Tathâgata, &c., named Padmaprabha, endowed with science and conduct, a Sugata, a knower of the world, an unsurpassed tamer of men, a master of gods and men, a Lord Buddha." Kern ch. III prose. Script compresses "&c.". Name glosses: Padmaprabha, "Flower Glow" (padma + prabha); his field Viraga, "Dispassion" (viraga). Kern leaves both names untranslated; the glosses are standard lexical renderings, flagged in audio as translations.
2. "Now that Tathâgata Padmaprabha, &c., Sâriputra, will preach the law by the instrumentality of three vehicles." Kern ch. III prose. Exact.
3. The Buddha-field description ("level, pleasant, delightful ... lapis lazuli ... checker-board of eight compartments distinguished by gold threads, each compartment having its jewel tree always and perpetually filled with blossoms and fruits of seven precious substances"). Kern ch. III prose. Script compresses; "perpetually in blossom and fruit" is a fair shortening of the clause.
4. "were so pleased, glad, charmed, thrilling with delight and joy, that they covered the Lord severally with their own robes." Kern ch. III prose. The full subject in the text is the long assembly list (the four classes plus gods, Nagas, goblins, Gandharvas, demons, Garudas, Kinnaras, great serpents, men and beings not human) "on hearing the announcement of the venerable Sâriputra's destiny to supreme, perfect enlightenment"; the script preserves that breadth.

### Chapter 3 (script) from Kern ch. VI

5. Kashyapa's prediction: homage to "thirty thousand kotis of Buddhas"; future Tathagata "by the name of Rasmiprabhâsa (i.e. beaming with rays)"; world "Avabhâsa (i.e. lustre)"; age "Mahâvyûha"; lifetime "twelve intermediate kalpas," true law "twenty intermediate kalpas," counterfeit as many. Kern ch. VI prose. Script compresses; Kern's own parenthetical glosses are the source of the translated names.
6. Subhuti: field "called Ratnasambhava and his epoch Ratnaprabhâsa." Kern ch. VI prose. Exact.
7. Katyayana: future Tathagata "named Gambûnada-prabhâsa (i.e. gold-shine)"; stupas "a thousand yoganas in height, fifty yoganas in circumference, and consisting of seven precious substances." Kern ch. VI prose. Exact. (The fourth prediction, to Maudgalyayana, gives stupas a thousand yojanas high and five hundred around; the script's stupa detail is attributed to Katyayana, correctly.)
8. The four recipients of ch. VI: Mahakashyapa, Subhuti, Mahakatyayana, Mahamaudgalyayana. Script compresses to "Kashyapa... Subhuti... Katyayana... Maudgalyayana gets his." Order and content verified.

### Chapter 4 (script) from Kern ch. IV

9. "the owner of much wealth and gold, treasures and granaries." Kern ch. IV prose. Exact.
10. "O how happy should I be, were my son to enjoy this mass of wealth!" Kern ch. IV prose. Exact. (The son disappeared "fifty years ago" per the same passage.)
11. "frightened, terrified, alarmed, seized with a feeling of horripilation all over the body, and agitated in mind." Kern ch. IV prose. Exact. "Horripilation: his hair stands on end" is the dictionary gloss (correct), spoken as explanation.
12. "I have given you no offence." Kern ch. IV prose. Exact.
13. "lays off his wreath and ornaments, parts with his soft, clean, and gorgeous attire, puts on dirty raiment, takes a basket in his right hand, smears his body with dust, and goes to his son." Kern ch. IV prose. Exact.

### Chapter 5 (script) from Kern ch. IV

14. "look upon me as if I were thy father." Kern ch. IV prose. Exact.
15. "though he continues taking his abode in the hovel of straw." Kern ch. IV prose. Exact.
16. "Hear, gentlemen! this is my own son, by me begotten." and "He is my son, I am his father. To him I leave all my revenues, and all my personal (or private) wealth shall he acknowledge (his own)." Kern ch. IV prose. Kern's parenthetical "(or private)" and "(his own)" flattened in audio.
17. "seeking for nothing but Nirvâna as our fee. We were content, O Lord, with the Nirvâna obtained, and thought to have gained much at the hands of the Tathâgata." Kern ch. IV prose (the elders' confession). Script compresses the tail.
18. The "twenty years" of employment: "employs him for the clearing of the heap of dirt during twenty years" (prose) and stanza 28, "employs him in his service for fully twenty years." Exact.

### Chapter 6 (script) from Kern ch. VIII

19. "After satiating him with nourishment, he gives him a jewel of great value. He ties it with a knot within the upper robe and feels satisfaction at having given that jewel." Kern ch. VIII, stanza 37. Exact.
20. "as to that jewel, he has forgotten it; he has not the slightest remembrance of its having been tied in his upper robe." Kern ch. VIII, stanza 39. Exact.
21. "This friend properly reprimands him and shows him the jewel within his robe." Kern ch. VIII, stanza 40. Exact.
22. "At this sight the man feels extremely happy." Kern ch. VIII, stanza 41. Exact.
23. Teller attribution: the parable is spoken by "the five hundred self-controlled (Arhats), Agnata-Kaundinya and the rest" (prose lead-in to the stanzas). The script's "the five hundred arhats spell it out" is correct. Their self-application: "the Tathâgata formerly, when he still followed the course of duty of a Bodhisattva, raised in us also ideas of omniscience, but we, O Lord, did not perceive, nor know it" (prose). Paraphrased in the script; no direct quote.

### Chapter 8 (script) from Kern ch. XIX

24. "when the reign (of the law) was being oppressed by proud monks." Kern ch. XIX prose. Exact.
25. "did not teach nor study; the only thing he did was, whenever he descried from afar a monk or nun, a male or female lay devotee, to approach them and exclaim: I do not contemn you, worthies. You deserve no contempt, for you all observe the course of duty of Bodhisattvas and are to become Tathâgatas." Kern ch. XIX prose. Exact. Note: the text gives the exclamation twice, once with "worthies," once with "sisters"; the audio uses "worthies." The name Sadâparibhûta: sada, always + paribhuta, despised/disparaged. The tradition's common rendering is "Never Disparaging" (after his practice); the script notes the mockers coined it as a jeer, i.e. "the Always Disparaged." Both renderings are standard.
26. "all were extremely irritated and angry at it, showed him their displeasure, abused and insulted him." Exact.
27. "Why does he, unasked, declare that he feels no contempt for us? just by so doing he shows a contempt for us." Exact.
28. "we do not care for what is not true." Exact.
29. "Many years ... went on during which that Bodhisattva ... was being abused, but he was not angry at anybody, nor felt malignity, and to those who, when he addressed them in the said manner, cast a clod or stick at him, he loudly exclaimed from afar: I do not contemn you." Exact (script splits into two quotes).
30. The era: Tathagata Bhishmagarjitasvararaja, eon Vinirbhoga, world Mahasambhava, "twenty hundred thousand myriads of kotis of Tathâgatas" of the same name in succession. Verified in Kern ch. XIX prose; names respelled for audio.

### Chapter 9 (script) from Kern ch. XIX

31. "happened to hear this Dharmaparyâya of the Lotus of the True Law when the end of his life was impending, and the moment of dying drawing near." Exact.
32. "the perfection of sight, hearing, smell, taste, body, and mind ... he at the same time made a vow to prolong his life for twenty hundred thousand myriads of kotis of years, and promulgated this Dharmaparyâya." Exact (script compresses the list introduction).
33. "That Bodhisattva then is myself, Sâkyamuni." Kern ch. XIX, stanza 7. Exact.
34. "And those persons who only believed in perception by the senses, those monks, nuns, male and female lay devotees who by the sage were admonished of enlightenment, / And who have seen many kotis of Buddhas, are the monks here before me,-no less than five hundred,-nuns, and female lay devotees." Kern ch. XIX, stanzas 8-9. The script's compression ("are the monks here before me, no less than five hundred") preserves the claim; the full stanza text is here for the record.
35. "he propitiated thousands of kotis of Buddhas" (stanza 6, "disappearing from that place, he propitiated thousands of kotis of Buddhas"). Exact.

## Honesty notes (published with the episode)

- All quotes verified word-for-word against the staged PD sources on 2026-11-22.
- Chapter numbering follows Kern's SBE21 order; Sadaparibhuta is ch. XIX there (XX in the Sanskrit and Chinese orders). Noted because listeners using other translations will find it one chapter later.
- Name glosses (Flower Glow, Dispassion, Beaming with Rays, Luster, gold-shine) follow Kern's own parenthetical glosses where he gives them, and standard lexical renderings where he does not (Padmaprabha, Viraga); both are flagged as translations in the audio.
- The script's interpretive frame (the wages as the phantom city, the jewel/robe comparison with Dzogchen) is original commentary and is flagged as such in the text.
- Narration: Aoede AI voice; pronunciation of Sanskrit and Tibetan terms is approximate.

- Final editorial check corrected one ambiguous sentence before publication: gold-shine is Katyayana's future Buddha-name, not his field's name. The affected take was requested again in full.

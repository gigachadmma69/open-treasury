# The Map That Ate the Country

Source companion. The Open Treasury. 19 November 2026.

Why a useful picture must leave something out

This course compares sources without making them agree. Fiction, historical reports, religious claims, ideal constructions and the narrator's own arguments remain different kinds of evidence. The reading record below names what was actually read and what was not.

## Reading record


1. **Lewis Carroll, Sylvie and Bruno Concluded**, chapter 11, The Man in the Moon. Primary literary text. Full map conversation and surrounding context read; chapter page obtained. Lit2Go's source identifies the 1893 Macmillan edition.
   - https://etc.usf.edu/lit2go/211/sylvie-and-bruno-concluded/4652/chapter-11-the-man-in-the-moon/
   - High confidence for the fictional map scene. Never say the map was spread out or that actual crops died beneath it.
2. **Carroll map excerpt**, Duke-hosted teaching page. Independent institutional presentation of the same primary scene, not an independent historical event.
   - https://people.duke.edu/~ng46/topics/lewis-carroll.htm
   - Used as cross-check, not as a second author agreeing with Carroll.
3. **Jonathan Swift, Gulliver's Travels**, part III, chapter V. Full chapter read at Standard Ebooks. Primary satire.
   - https://standardebooks.org/ebooks/jonathan-swift/gullivers-travels/text/chapter-3-5
   - High confidence for the language project. Narrator and satirical author are not interchangeable. Avoid adopting the narrator's contempt for women or ordinary people, and omit unrelated animal-harm/disability material.
4. **Josiah Royce, The World and the Individual, First Series**, Supplementary Essay, Section III, Theory of the Sources and Consequences of Any Recurrent Operation of Thought. Primary philosophy, Gifford Archives transcription.
   - https://giffordarchives.org/books/world-and-individual-vol-1/section-iii-theory-sources-and-consequences-any-recurrent-operation
   - Read the map definition, England example, exact physical-construction caveat, process/completed-whole distinction, and beginning of self-representative-system definition. Not a full-book or full-essay reading claim.
   - High confidence for claims used. Ideal assumptions must remain distinct from physically accomplished objects and metaphysical proof.
5. **Gifford Archives book overview, The World and the Individual, Vol. 1**. Institutional contextual record.
   - https://giffordarchives.org/books/world-and-individual-vol-1
   - Used to situate the metaphysical work, not to substitute a summary for the primary passage.
6. **Eric Steinhart, Royce's Model of the Absolute**, author-hosted scholarly study. Introduction and map/structure discussion read, with references to Royce pp. 506-509. No bibliographic date inferred where not verified.
   - https://ericsteinhart.com/articles/royceinfinity.pdf
   - Supports the constructive/model role of the map and distinction between formal articulation and the reality claim. No quotation or reproduction of diagrams.
7. **Models in Science**, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. First published 27 February 2006; substantive revision 2 April 2025 displayed on the page. Scholarly survey. Relevant representation, idealization, abstraction and distortion passages read selectively.
   - https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/models-science/
   - High confidence for existence of the distinctions and debates. Not a claim all models work, all omitted factors are irrelevant, or one account of idealization commands agreement.

The seven source records provide primary fiction, primary philosophy, institutional context/cross-checks and modern scholarship. These are not seven independent witnesses to one thesis. The manuscript makes a comparison, not a historical transmission claim.

## Quotation and attribution check

No passage of modern scholarship or modern translation is directly quoted. Most source scenes are paraphrased. A few ordinary short phrases and the historical fictional conceit, including the country's doing nearly as well as its own map, remain recognizable from Carroll and must retain the chapter credit. There is no quotation from Borges and no claim about historical influence between these writers.

The script's invented dialogue, questions and examples are not attributed to the authors. No quotation marks are used to turn the narrator's synthesis into an alleged historical aphorism. Heading phrases such as the map eating the country are original framing, not a source quotation.

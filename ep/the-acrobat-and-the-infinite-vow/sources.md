# The Acrobat and the Infinite Vow
## Source, quotation, claim and rights ledger v1

16 November 2026. Covers RECOGNITION-SCRIPT-v1.md only. This is the editorial record, not a claim of completed narration or release.

## Scope and result

The question is whether recognizing reality should precede compassionate work, and whether universal bodhisattva vows necessarily postpone Buddhahood. The script's answer is deliberately qualified:

- Awakening before teaching is explicit in MN 26. It is not a requirement that ordinary people withhold useful help until fully awakened.
- An undiscoverable beginning of samsara does not establish an unavoidable future. SN 15.9 urges release, not hopelessness. The mathematical observation in chapter 4 is an original logical illustration, not a claim that a mathematical model proves Buddhist cosmology.
- Explicit others-first/postponement language exists in the Ksitigarbha Sutra. It must not be erased by saying that all such language is a misunderstanding.
- Continued service after unexcelled awakening is explicit in the Precious Garland verse as quoted by Thubten Chodron. This is a different formulation, not a correction silently imposed on the Ksitigarbha text.
- Universal liberation and relinquishing fixed notions of beings coexist in Diamond Sutra sections 3-4. Empty does not mean that care can be abandoned.
- Avalokiteshvara teaches emptiness in the Heart Sutra while called bodhisattva. Sources differ about his status; the scholarly survey is identified as secondary evidence.
- Public Dzogchen teachings distinguish recognition, training and stable realization, and include compassion in the path. Public Mahamudra teaching uses ordinary mind in a technical sense, not as endorsement of every habit.
- Humor and ordinary bodily life are compatible with seriousness. Neither tears nor a good joke establish attainment.

No individual contemporary disputant is named, described through identifying social-media details, mocked, or assessed for attainment. No autobiographical attribution is given to the eating/letting-go image. No claimed teacher identification from uncertain speech is used.

### S1. Sedaka Sutta / At Sedaka (The Acrobat), SN 47.19

- URL: https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN47_19.html
- Translation: Thanissaro Bhikkhu, dhammatalks.org. Primary canonical discourse in modern translation. Page publication date not displayed in fetched text.
- Read: complete short discourse, including both directions of reciprocal protection and the concluding explanation.
- Textual detail: bamboo acrobat and assistant translated as Frying Pan. The assistant stands on the master's shoulders on the pole. The master initially proposes watching each other; the assistant proposes each attending to self. The Buddha approves the assistant in that case, then explicitly connects self-protection and protection of others. Cultivation/development explains the first direction; endurance, harmlessness, goodwill and sympathy explain the second.
- Use: chapters 1, 11, 12. Entire scene is paraphrased. Do not cut the second direction and leave an apparent endorsement of selfishness. The relationship to contemporary boundaries and finite commitments is our interpretation, not an ancient clinical theory.
- Rights: no extended translation quotation; no image, recording or page design reproduced.

### S3. The Stick, SN 15.9

- URL: https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN15_9.html
- Translation: Thanissaro Bhikkhu, dhammatalks.org. Complete short discourse read.

## Contradictions preserved

1. Ksitigarbha's literal sequence versus the continued-service-after-awakening formulation in Precious Garland. Both are left visible.
2. Recognition of already-present nature versus continuing training and stable realization. No assertion that every glimpse is final.
3. Universal vows versus finite capacity. The course offers a practical synthesis labeled as its own, not a direct ancient quotation.
4. Emotional ease versus tears. Neither is turned into an attainment test.
5. Different accounts of Avalokiteshvara's status. Source diversity is not made into a universal Vajrayana claim.
6. Beginningless wandering versus possible release. No argument for eternal compulsory suffering is smuggled into beginninglessness.

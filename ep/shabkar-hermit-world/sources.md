# Shabkar: The Hermit Who Could Not Ignore the World
Released 3 November 2026. Episode 103. Narrated by Tara, an AI voice; narration produced with Muse. 69:09, eight chapters, 9,415 spoken words.

## What this is
A bounded study of Shabkar's life and four songs: When Remembering My Mother, Song of Compassion, Song of Nonsectarianism, and The Sunlight of Pure Perception. Twenty-three selected rendered passages are interleaved with original commentary. Not a complete autobiography, collected works, or certified scholarly translation. No advanced practice procedures, empowerment, or transmission are offered. The songs' Buddhist premises are presented as their premises, not proven empirical facts. The severe mother-song admonition is Shabkar addressing himself, not advice for a grieving listener.

## Source and method
The renderings are comparison-informed working English versions of the Lotsawa House Tibetan texts, audited against Adam Pearcey's and Rachel Pang's published translations. Those translations were read before and during drafting. No blind Tibetan-first or wholly independent wording claim is made. Some short literal phrases coincide; they retain the Lotsawa House attribution and BY-NC terms. Selection, translation, prose formatting and interleaved commentary are changes. The marked script and quote ledger identify every rendered passage, full Tibetan, source URL, and comparison. The modern titles of the two Pang songs were supplied by their translator.

Life scenes are mediated by Becky Best's Columbia-hosted essay on Matthieu Ricard's autobiography translation. We did not read the entire autobiography or Ricard's book for this episode, and do not read its translated quotations. Best's account runs through age fifty-six; later life is outside this episode's checked record. Autobiography-reported events are distinguished from independent history and our interpretation. The working dates are 1781-1851, following Lotsawa House, Columbia and Treasury of Lives; Tony Duff's official publisher page gives 1850 as a variant. No Duff book text was used.

Rachel H. Pang's research supplies the interpretive frame of impartiality. The episode's selection of three strands is ours; her range is wider. The 2014 paper's introduction and the 2015 paper's abstract, introduction, semantic-range and basic-attitude/practice sections, plus a conclusion excerpt, were checked, not every page of both papers. No translated extracts or scholarly prose from them is narrated. Reports of Patrul trying to meet Shabkar are not a verified meeting; absence of identified formal contact is not proof no contact occurred. Shabkar is not declared founder of a centralized Rime organization.

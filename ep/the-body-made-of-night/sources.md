# The Body Made of Night: source ledger and editorial notes

Prepared 23 November 2026. Sources were searched and the substantive pages below fetched and read for this course. All narration is original commentary and paraphrase. ## Scientific evidence

1. Benjamin Baird, Sergio A. Mota-Rolim, Martin Dresler, "The cognitive neuroscience of lucid dreaming," Neuroscience and Biobehavioral Reviews 100 (2019), 305-323.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6451677/
Peer-reviewed review. Supports definition, physiological validation, eye-signaling methods, and limits of neurobiological evidence. The review notes small samples and mixed findings. It does not show consciousness without brain activity. High confidence for bounded claims, not an assertion that this is the newest exhaustive review.

2. National Science Foundation, report on Konkoly and colleagues' 2021 multi-laboratory study, 18 February 2021.
https://www.nsf.gov/science-matters/scientists-break-through-wall-sleep-untapped-world-dreams
Institutional research account. Supports two-way communication during monitored REM sleep, simple arithmetic/questions, eye/facial signals, limits of success. High confidence. Primary article was located but its PubMed fetch returned no content, so the narration uses the corroborating institutional account and does not supply precise response percentages.
Primary study discovered: https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/s0960-9822(21)00059-2
PubMed record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33607035/

3. Tan et al., "A systematic review of new empirical data on lucid dream induction techniques," Journal of Sleep Research, first published 21 November 2022, volume publication 2023.
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jsr.13786
Peer-reviewed review. Supports that MILD was the most effective method among those assessed, with variable study quality and a need for replication. Does not support guaranteeing success or importing laboratory efficacy to our bedtime adaptation. High confidence.

4. "Benefits and concerns of seeking and experiencing lucid dreams: benefits are tied to successful induction and dream control," SLEEP Advances 3 (2022), zpac027.
https://academic.oup.com/sleepadvances/article/3/1/zpac027/6693839
Peer-reviewed observational content analysis of online posts. Supports reported benefits and concerns, including poor sleep, dysphoric dreams, paralysis and reality confusion. Not a representative prevalence estimate, clinical safety trial, or demonstration of causality. Course gives conservative stop criteria, not medical treatment claims. Moderate confidence for reported concerns; no incidence statistics used.

## Tibetan and Bön teachings, public sources

5. Geshe Tenzin Wangyal Rinpoche, "How to Practice Dream Yoga," Lion's Roar, 4 February 2025.
https://www.lionsroar.com/is-this-a-dream/
Teacher-authored public teaching. Supports day/night continuity, four foundations, concentration, intention, recall, transforming habitual reactions and the distinction between lucid dreaming and broader dream/sleep/bardo training. High confidence for attribution to this public presentation. Course summarizes the foundations and does NOT reproduce the article's chakra/lotus procedure or advanced methods. General exercises are labeled adaptations.

6. Rubin Museum, "In Your Dreams: Buddhist Sleep Practices," 16 August 2016.
https://rubinmuseum.org/in-your-dreams-buddhist-sleep-practices/
Museum educational overview. Supports relation of dream yoga to Tilopa/Naropa, Six Yogas, illusory body and clear light. High confidence for broad historical framework, not sufficient for a detailed practice manual. Precise lists and arrangements may vary, acknowledged in narration.

7. Tulku Urgyen, As It Is, Volume I, official publisher description.
https://www.rangjung.com/book_title/as-it-is-volume-i-2/
Supports published teaching emphasis on empty cognizance and recognition. A short official excerpt was read but is paraphrased, not quoted, in the course.
Bibliographic corroboration, Erik Pema Kunsang translation:
https://books.google.com/books/about/As_it_is.html?id=7lKZXzQByhEC

8. Tsoknyi Rinpoche, "The Two Truths ~ Indivisible," Pundarika Foundation.
https://tsoknyirinpoche.org/two-truths/
Teacher's official foundation. Supports dreamlike appearance as dependent and empty of inherent existence, functioning relative personhood, relationships and compassion, and inseparability of the two truths. Narration uses original explanation rather than quoted teaching. High confidence.

9. Shambhala publisher's excerpt, "The Tibetan Yogas of Dream and Sleep."
https://www.shambhala.com/blogs/snow-lion-newsletter-archive/the-tibetan-yogas-of-dream-and-sleep
Corroborating authorized publisher source for Tenzin Wangyal's broader framing. No licensed translation or extended text reproduced.

10. Shambhala publisher's public account, "Dream Yoga and the Practice of Natural Light."
https://www.shambhala.com/blogs/snow-lion-newsletter-archive/dream-yoga-and-the-practice-of-natural-light
Context for Dzogchen-related dream practice. No additional technical instructions imported.

### The seven-recognitions limit

A forum comment attributes a passage to As It Is vol. I pp.156-158 about recognizing seven times in dreams and recognition in the bardo. This is a lead only, NOT primary verification:
https://www.reddit.com/r/TibetanBuddhism/comments/1ikvkh2/from_lucid_dreaming_and_dream_yoga_to_where/
The publisher page and Google Books page do not expose those pages. Pirated scans encountered in searches were not opened. The course openly states this limit, does not quote the forum text, does not call seven ordinary lucid dreams a liberation guarantee, and invents no traditional seven-stage list. Chapter 17's seven QUESTIONS are explicitly original and explicitly not Tulku Urgyen's recognitions. A lawful edition and teacher familiar with the passage could resolve this later. No paid book purchased.

## Philosophy, history, anthropology

18. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Yogacara."
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/yogacara/
Academic reference. Supports disagreement over ontological interpretations, consciousness not straightforwardly a personal/universal mental substance, and critiques of simple solipsism. High confidence for overview, not a definitive resolution of the scholarly debate.

19. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Dreams and Dreaming."
https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dreams-dreaming/
Academic context for philosophical problems, epistemology and historical discussions. Supports keeping phenomenology, epistemology and ontology distinct. Narration's argument is original synthesis, not represented as a settled scientific result.

20. Aristotle, On Dreams, J. I. Beare translation, Internet Classics Archive at MIT.
https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/dreams.html
Public primary text. Recognition during sleep discussed in part 3. No direct quotation included. Ancient physiology explicitly not treated as current science. No fabricated Greek induction technique offered.

21. British Museum, Papyrus Chester Beatty III, sheet 1, EA10683,1.
https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA10683-1
Official object record: dream book, 19th Dynasty, circa 1220 BC. High confidence. Evidence for dream interpretation, not proof of a lucid-dream curriculum. No object image used.

22. Kevin P. Groark, "Between Dreams and Realities: Oneiric Culture among the Tzotzil Maya of San Juan Chamula in the Highlands of Chiapas," EntreDiversidades, 2017.
https://entrediversidades.unach.mx/index.php/entrediversidades/article/download/12/33/
Peer-reviewed ethnography. Supports culturally specific social and interpersonal roles of dreams. Contemporary Tzotzil Maya evidence is NOT presented as an ancient pan-Maya practice or objective verification of extrasomatic travel. No text translated or quoted.

23. Alessandro Lupo, "Tlacochitta, Seeing Things while Asleep: Utterances and Reflections on the Dream Practices of the Nahua," Estudios de Cultura Nahuatl 63 (2022), 157-192.
https://nahuatl.historicas.unam.mx/index.php/ecn/article/download/77991/69093
Peer-reviewed open-access ethnography/history. Distinguishes pre-Hispanic Mexica evidence via colonial sources from contemporary Cuetzalan Nahua research. Supports ritual vocation, knowledge and agency themes as reported cultural understandings. Underlying paper carries BY-NC-ND; no text, figures or translated passage reproduced or adapted, only factual discussion with citation. No generic Aztec exercise offered.

24. Sophie Chao, "Dreams," Open Encyclopedia of Anthropology, 8 August 2024.
https://www.anthroencyclopedia.com/entry/dreams
Academic survey. Supports diversity of cultural concepts of dreaming, morality, social relations, knowledge and interpretive authority. Course treats difference as meaningful and does not flatten shamanism into one tradition.

25. "The Dreaming," Working with Indigenous Australians.
http://workingwithindigenousaustralians.info/content/Culture_2_The_Dreaming.html
Educational context for ancestral law, country and responsibility rather than simple private sleep imagery. Supplementary general overview, not Indigenous permission to reproduce ceremony. Course supplies no Aboriginal ritual and explicitly avoids universalizing across peoples.

## Claims that remain interpretation

- Dreams offer counterexamples to equating experienced vividness with literal external existence exactly as it appears.
- Recognition within an ongoing display can model a changed relation to thought and emotion.
- Training might transfer to less waking reactivity, but this must be observed rather than assumed.
- No inference from REM lucidity to consciousness without a brain, postmortem survival, rebirth, metaphysical idealism, or Dzogchen realization is presented as scientifically proved.
- A waking person and a dream character are NOT treated as ethically interchangeable, and waking physical consequences remain binding.

## Practice scope

Public, non-forceful attention/recall/intention exercises and our own clearly labeled adaptations only. No supplements, galantamine, breath retention, channel manipulation, inner-heat instruction, deprivation, deprivation-by-alarm, private teachings, or initiation material. Avoidance of adverse sleep/mental-health effects takes priority over inducing lucidity. Clinical cautions are conservative boundaries, not diagnosis or claims that all contemplative practice is dangerous.
